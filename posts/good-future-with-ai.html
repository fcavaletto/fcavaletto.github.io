What would a good future with AI actually look like?

20 November 2026

When people talk about AI going well, the picture is usually something like this: nobody has to work anymore, every disease is cured, and you can have whatever you want whenever you want it. I used to find that convincing and even somewhat alluring, but the more I thought about it the less I liked it. If everything is done for you and there is nothing left to try or get better at, I'm not sure what you would actually do all day.

I'm not the first person to notice this. Eliezer Yudkowsky wrote a set of essays on LessWrong called the Fun Theory sequence about exactly this problem. His main point is that a lot of imagined paradises would be pretty miserable to live in, because they take away the things that make life interesting. Reading it made me try to write down what I would want a good future to look like:

  • Making my own decisions. Even if an AI could work out better than me where I should live or which job would make me happiest, I would still want to be the one who decides.
  • Having problems worth working on. A lot of what I've enjoyed most came from things that were hard at first, like learning maths or getting a piece of code to work after hours of trying. If every problem were solved before you even noticed it, you would lose that sense of accomplishment.
  • Other people. AI could help with a lot of things, but I don't want a world where people mostly talk to machines because machines are always easier to talk to. People are messy, and dealing with friends and family matters partly because the interactions are not always easy.
  • Being able to keep learning and changing. A good life isn't doing the same pleasant thing forever. It's becoming a slightly different person as you go and finding new things interesting along the way.
  • The physical world: going for a walk, cooking for friends, being tired after a long day outside. I don't think a good future is one where we all live inside a screen.

However, there is one thing I am looking forward to leaving behind, and that is unnecessary suffering. I mean illnesses, poverty, and jobs that wear people down only because nobody has found a better way to do them. This is where AI could really help, and Dario Amodei's essay Machines of Loving Grace gives a detailed picture of what that could look like in medicine and science.

I'm writing about this because I don't think we are going to get a good future by default. It's easy to imagine AI systems that are very good at keeping us busy and satisfied while we slowly lose our agency, and the risk is that we will realize what's happening too late. We are also building more and more capable systems without good ways to check what they are actually doing or what they are trying to achieve, and I find it worrying how little attention this gets.

I think the possible upside is big enough that it's worth real effort to get this right, and that's a big part of why I care about AI safety.

What would you add/remove from my list?
